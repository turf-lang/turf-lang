You marked your struct's constructor as static, but it can't be

`new` is the method that sets up a struct's fields when it's constructed -- its entire job is to fill in `self`. The `static` keyword means "this method has no `self` at all," which is the exact opposite of what `new` needs, so Turf won't let the two be combined.

Here's a `new` declared correctly, with no `static`:

    fn Point.new(x: int, y: int): void {
        self.x = x;
        self.y = y;
    }

Adding `static` to that same declaration is what triggers this error:

    fn Point.new(x: int, y: int) static: void {   // ERROR: 'new' can't be 'static'
        self.x = x;
        self.y = y;
    }

The fix is simple: just delete the `static` keyword from the declaration. `new` is always allowed to use `self`, because filling in `self` is the whole point of having a constructor in the first place.
